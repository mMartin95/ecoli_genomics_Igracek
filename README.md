# Genomic analyses of E. coli strains repeatedly exposed to zinc (ZnO, ZnONPs) and strains with withdrawal

Variant validation, structural variation (SV) pipeline, and methodological impact assessment for *Escherichia coli* strains subjected to long-term sub-inhibitory zinc stress up to the 40th subculture (ZnO40, ZnONPs40),  withdrawal lineages (ZnO20+20, ZnONPs20+20), and parallel control (C40). 

Phenotypic screening revealed an unexpected emergence of **antibiotic cross-resistance**, wherein continuous exposure to bulk zinc oxide (ZnO40) drove strain to reach the EUCAST clinical resistance breakpoints for key therapeutic classes, notably cefazolin (CFZ), gentamicin (GEN), and amikacin (AMK). 

Conversely, continuous exposure to zinc oxide nanoparticles (ZnONPs40) resulted in a paradoxical hypersensitivity, increasing bacterial susceptibility to gentamicin and amikacin.
---


## Project overview

This repository contains a comprehensive variant calling (VC) and structural variation (SV) analysis pipeline designed to uncover the **genetic drivers of zinc tolerance and cross-resistance** in *E. coli*. 

By tracking genomic trajectories across prolonged heavy metal exposure and subsequent pressure withdrawal, this project investigates whether the observed antibiotic resistance phenotypes are underpinned by fixed genomic mutations (like hard selective sweeps in regulatory networks or efflux systems) or transient physiological adaptations.

A complete graphical scheme of the experimental evolution design is provided in `Igracek_scheme.png`.

## Processing of the samples:

Samples of E. coli strains - **C40, ZnO40, ZnONPs40, ZnO20+20, ZnONPs20+20** were processed.

The sequencing datasets underwent the following processing pipeline:
- **Quality control**: Checked using `FastQC` (identifying adapter content and low-quality 3' ends).
- **Trimming**: Processed using `fastp` (minimum length 50 bp, minimum quality score Q20).
- **Quality control of trimmed reads**: Checked using `FastQC` and HTML reports of both raw and trimmed reads compiled using `MultiQC`. 
- **Alignment**: Mapped against the *E. coli* ATCC 25922 reference genome using `BWA-MEM` or `Bowtie2` by `breseq`. Resulting SAM files were converted to BAM files, filtered, and indexed using `samtools`.
- **Alignment metrics**: 
  - *Mapping efficiency* - Achieved a high overall read-mapping rate: sample C40 (control) **95.84%**, ZnO40 **93.93%**, ZnONPs40 **96.77%**, ZnO20+20 **95.7%**, and ZnONPs20+20 **94.8%**.
  - *Mapping coverage & Depth* - Achieved near-complete genome coverage (**>99.89%**) with a stable mean sequencing depth ranging between **53× and 76×** across the circular reference chromosome (`NZ_CP009072`).
- **Duplicate removal and methodological control**: Evaluated using Picard `MarkDuplicates`. Consequently, unfiltered sorted BAMs were retained for structural variant analysis.
- **Variant calling and annotation**: 
  - **SNPs and indels**: Generated using `bcftools mpileup | bcftools call`, evaluated individually and jointly across all samples, and filtered using `QUAL > 30 && DP >= 10 && MQ > 30`. Variants were annotated using `snpEff` against the local `ATCC 25922` database.
  - **Structural variants (SVs)**: Detected using `breseq` (identifying large deletions, and new junctions) utilizing the annotated `NZ_CP009072` reference sequence.

### Structural variants and & Mobile Element Analysis

Based on the joint variant calling by `bcftools/breseq`, filtration of samples and comparison of adapted strain (ZnO40, ZnONPs40, ZnO20+20, ZnONPs20+20) and control sample (C40), the each adapted strain contained fixed mutations.

While standard variant calling (`bcftools`and `snpEff`) accurately captured point mutations and small indels, large-scale genomic rearrangements, such as multi-kilobase deletions and insertion sequences (IS, transpositions) were resolved using **`breseq`** (v0.40.2) followed by custom coordinate-based filtering with **`bedtools`**.

To eliminate false-positive results caused by minor coordinate shifts or sequencing noise, all candidate regions were validated directly against the aligned read data (reference.bam generated by `Bowtie2` via `breseq` tool) using `bedtools coverage`. Because deletions and insertions leave opposite alignment footprints, inverse validation criteria were applied.

> **Note on Reference Annotation Identifiers:** Functional annotations from the `snpEff` pipeline reference the strain assembly utilizing `K758_RS` locus tags, whereas structural variants resolved via `breseq` utilize the updated RefSeq `NZ_CP009072` annotation (`DR76_RS` locus tags). Both identifiers correspond to the same *E. coli* ATCC 25922 reference genome.

## Molecular mechanisms and phenotypic impact

* **Bulk ZnO Adaptation (`ZnO40`):** 
  * *Observed mutations:* Missense mutation in the envelope stress sensor kinase **`cpxA`** (p.Leu173Pro) and lytic murein transglycosylase **`mltF`**.
  * *Biological implication:* The CpxAR system acts as the primary envelope integrity guardian. Its modification alters membrane permeability and stress signaling, which likely serves as a defense against intracellular zinc influx. This structural fortification inadvertently acts as a bridge to **clinical co-resistance** against aminoglycosides (gentamicin, amikacin) and cephalosporins (cefazolin) by restricting drug entry or altering surface targets.

* **Nano-ZnO Stress Response (`ZnONPs40`):**
  * *Observed mutation:* A massive **5.7 kb deletion** eliminating the Type VI Secretion System (T6SS) locus (6 genes, including *vgr* and *paar* components).
  * *Biological implication:* Nano-sized zinc generates severe internal oxidative stress and membrane friction. To survive, the cell undergoes strict metabolic triage—sacrificing its energy-expensive interbacterial "harpoon gun" (T6SS) and virulence machinery to conserve ATP and macromolecular resources strictly required for baseline survival.
  
* *Note on Intergenic Variants:* The functional impact of identified intergenic mutations remains putative; confirming whether these regulatory shifts drive differential gene expression requires subsequent transcriptomic (RNA-Seq) validation.
